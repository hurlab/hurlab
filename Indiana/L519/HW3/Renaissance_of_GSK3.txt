The protein Glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) has a diverse range of substrates. Many of them require preceding phosphorylation of a site four residues away from the phosphorylation site of GSK3. This is called priming phosphorylation. The closest homolog of GSK3 is mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK). They share many active sites and structural similarities. 
GSK3 itself can be phosphorylated to an inactive form. The phosphorylation of serine residue of amino terminus of GSK3 turns this portion to pseudo-substrate such that it blocks access of other substrates to the active site and so inhibits GSK3.

GSK3 was initially thought to be functionally confined to glycogen metabolism. Its involvement in many other pathways has been discovered over the course of time. Most representative ones are as follows:

1.	Insulin signaling pathway
2.	Growth factor signaling and cell division
3.	Cell fate specification during embryo development
4.	Microtubule function and apoptosis


1.	Insulin signaling pathway
GSK3 inhibits glycogen synthase by phosphorylation. GSK3 itself can be inhibited by a pathway that is initiated by cell insulin receptor. Protein kinase B(PKB), 3-phsphoinositide-dependent protein kinase 1 (PDK1) and phosphatidyl inositol-3 kinase (PI3K) mediate the signaling process. When GSK3 is deactivated, its substrates such as glycogen synthase and eukaryotic initiation factor 2B (eIF2B) become dephosphorylated by phosphatases. They then become involved in insulin-induced glycogen and protein synthesis.

2.	Growth factors and cell division
Cyclin D1 and c myc transcription factor can be phosphorylated by GSK3. These are then ubiquitylated and destroyed. Growth factor phosphorylates and deactivates GSK3. Amino acids can also reduce GSK3 activity through a pathway that involves rapamycin. When GSK3 is deactivated, CD1 and cMYC are activated, which then activate CDK4 and CDK6. These then inactivate the retinoblastoma gene product, and then entry into S-phase of cell cycle is facilitated. 
GSK3 can also deactivate c-jun by phosphorylation. The inhibition of GSK3 enhances c-jun activity which then enhances cyclin D1 transcription level.

3.	Cell fate specification during embryo development
In D.Melanogaster, wingless and zeste-white3 proteins are involved in development of alternating bristle and cuticle. Wingless suppresses GSK3 activity. An inactive mutant GSK3 experiment in xenopus laevis resulted in duplication of the dorsal axis. The participation of GSK3 in WNT signaling pathway is through a multi-protein complex that includes axin, beta-catenin and adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) protein. Compared with other substrates of GSK3, axin, APC and beta-catenin do not possess priming phosphate sites. The vertebrate homologue of wingless is WNT. If WNT is absent, GSK3 phosphorylates beta-catenin for proteolysis. 
The presence of WNT disrupts GSK3 activity on these proteins. The mechanism is still under investigation but is thought to involve another protein complex that includes dishellved (DVL) and frequently-rearranged-in-advanced-T-cell lymphomas protein (FRAT). When GSK3 is inactive, beta-catenin stabilizes, accumulates and translocates to the nucleus to interact with T-cell factors. T-cell factors then become involved in cell-fate specification pathway.

4.	Microtubule formation and apoptosis
Tau is a protein that stabilizes and polymerizes microtubules. GSK3 can efficiently phosphorylate 
tau at many sites. Tau has been found in an abnormally hyperphophorylated form in Alzheimer¡¯s disease and several other neurodegenerative diseases. Hyperphosphorylation of tau inhibits its binding to microtubule and the increased free pool of tau particles self-assemble into insoluble filament. An experiment using GSK3 inhibitors such as lithium and insulin found that dephosphorylation of Tau can be induced. 
Removal of survival factors from the medium or lowering the extracellular concentration of potassium ions can induce apoptosis of neuronal cells. GSK3 inhibitors can prevent apoptosis. This implies a role for GSK3 in cell apoptosis. It also indicates the therapeutic potential of GSK3 inhibitors to prevent neuronal apoptosis after stroke. 

*Conclusion
GSK3 functionality was long thought to be confined to glycogen metabolism. Gradually, its role in many other pathways has been found. Insulin signaling pathway, growth factor signaling and cell division, cell fate specification during embryo development, microtubule function, and apoptosis are representative pathways in which GSK3 participate. Its main functionality is inhibition of other protein activity by phosphorylation. GSK3 itself can be phosphorylated by inhibitors including lithium and insulin.      
In the post-genomic era where sequencing is performed routinely and many novel genes are found, identification of their functions is an important task. The GSK3 gene demonstrates that our current understanding of many pathways is still rudimentary, and improvement in methodologies and utilization of reagents is necessary for more efficient, further experiments.
